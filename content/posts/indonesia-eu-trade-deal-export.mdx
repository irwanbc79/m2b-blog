**Game-changing news for Indonesian exporters:** On September 23, 2025, Indonesia and the European Union finalized the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) – the most ambitious trade deal ever reached between the EU and a Southeast Asian economy. After nearly a decade of negotiations, this historic agreement is set to transform the export landscape for Indonesian businesses.

If you've been eyeing the European market or already exporting there, here's everything you need to know about how this deal will impact your business strategy.

## The Big Picture: What Just Changed?

The Indonesia-EU CEPA represents a massive win for Indonesian exporters. Here's what makes this deal groundbreaking:

**Near-Total Tariff Elimination:** Over 98% of tariff lines will be liberalized, covering close to 100% of trade value. That means virtually all your products will enter the EU market duty-free or with significantly reduced tariffs.

**Immediate Impact:** A whopping 80% of tariffs will be eliminated right when the agreement enters into force (expected in early 2027), with 96% liberalization achieved within just 5 years.

**Sectors That Win Big:**

- **Palm Oil** – EU market access with preferential treatment

- **Textiles & Footwear** – Critical alternative to US markets

- **Agri-Food Products** – Fisheries, processed foods, and more

- **Manufacturing** – Electronics, furniture, and industrial goods

## Why This Matters NOW

This isn't just another trade agreement – the timing is critical. With US tariffs hitting Indonesia at 32% and global supply chains fragmenting, the EU market offers a strategic lifeline and growth opportunity that smart exporters are already positioning for.

The EU represents a market of 450 million consumers with high purchasing power. For context, bilateral trade between Indonesia and the EU reached €27.3 billion in 2024, and this deal is projected to boost that by 15-30% within the first few years.

## The Fine Print: What You Need to Prepare For

While the opportunities are enormous, success requires preparation. Here are the key areas you need to focus on:

### 1. **Customs & Trade Facilitation**

The agreement introduces streamlined customs procedures that will get your goods through EU borders faster and more efficiently. Key features include:

- Self-certification system for tariff benefits (no more expensive origin certificates from third parties)

- Reduced documentation requirements

- Enhanced cooperation between Indonesian and EU customs authorities

- Transparent, predictable processes

**Action Item:** Start reviewing your current customs documentation processes now. The simpler procedures mean cost savings, but you need proper systems in place.

### 2. **Technical Standards & Certification**

This is HUGE for exporters in electronics, machinery, and energy-efficient products:

- Indonesia has agreed to accept certificates and test reports from EU-based accredited bodies

- No more expensive and time-consuming re-testing in Indonesia for certain sectors

- Harmonized labeling requirements – you can use internationally accepted symbols

- 60-day minimum comment period for any new technical regulations

**What This Means:** Lower compliance costs, faster market entry, and reduced regulatory uncertainty.

### 3. **Sustainability Requirements**

The agreement includes binding commitments on environmental and labor standards. This isn't optional – it's built into the deal:

- Paris Agreement climate commitments

- Forest conservation and biodiversity protection

- Sustainable palm oil production standards (especially relevant given EU Deforestation Regulation)

- ILO core labor rights principles

**Critical Note:** The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) remains in effect, though special considerations may apply for CEPA partner countries. If you export palm oil, timber, soy, rubber, cattle products, or related goods, compliance with EUDR is mandatory.

### 4. **Rules of Origin**

To benefit from preferential tariffs, your products must meet specific rules of origin criteria. This determines whether your goods qualify as "originating" from Indonesia.

**Pro Tip:** Work with a trade compliance specialist to ensure your products meet origin requirements. Even small changes in your supply chain can affect eligibility.

## Sector-Specific Opportunities

### **Textiles & Footwear Exporters**

With the US textile market facing uncertainty (Indonesia exported $7.7 billion in textiles to the US in 2023), the EU offers crucial market diversification. The CEPA eliminates duties on Indonesian textile and footwear exports, providing immediate competitiveness against other suppliers.

### **Automotive & Manufacturing**

Indonesia will eliminate its current 50% tariffs on EU vehicles over 5 years, while EU removes duties on Indonesian automotive components. If you're in the EV supply chain, local content requirements for certain sectors are being lifted.

### **Palm Oil & Agricultural Products**

Despite past tensions over deforestation regulations, the CEPA provides Indonesian palm oil producers with preferential EU market access. However, sustainability certification will be paramount.

### **Food & Beverage**

EU duties on Indonesian fisheries, processed foods, and beverages are being eliminated. Given the EU's love for exotic and quality food products, this sector has significant untapped potential.
